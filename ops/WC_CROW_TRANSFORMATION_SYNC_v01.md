## Decision

Lock the front-facing human figure as **Crow Heyoka’s unveiled form for the Transformation arc only**. It is not the default character model, not the mascot, and not a general-purpose portrait. The crow-headed version remains the primary seek-and-find mask/aspect across puzzles, posters, cards, and early campaign layers.

## Canon Function — Unveiled Form

Crow Heyoka’s unveiled face appears only after the thirteenth station is completed. Until then, the seeker meets him through masks: crow-head, shadow, jacket, hat, tail, feather, omen, and misdirection. The face underneath is not a simple reveal; it is the cost of reconstruction. When it appears, it confirms that the search was never for a missing man alone. It was for the part of the seeker that survived the ritual, learned to wear the wound without worshiping it, and became visible only after choosing transformation over disappearance.

## Transformation Symbol Rule

The gold phoenix on the coat is **additive canon**, not a replacement for Crow. It marks the unveiled form after integration.  
Use rule: **Crow = witness / fragment / seeker. Phoenix = consequence / renewal / visible return.**  
Never replace the crow emblem globally with the phoenix.

## Style Bible

Primary texture: textured painterly gothic realism with controlled grit. Surfaces should feel worn, hand-touched, fog-aged, and physically printable. Avoid slick fantasy gloss, cartoon flattening, fake glitter, AI shine, UI artifacts, and decorative excess that does not serve the search.

Color keys: cool desaturated base — smoke blue, charcoal, bruised green-gray, old black leather, bone parchment. Warm accent points — tarnished gold, lantern amber, candlelight, moonlit brass, small red only when signaling threat or cost.

Shape language: Crow reads through silhouette first — wide-brim hat or beanie, leather/sherpa jacket, striped shirt, boots, hidden tail/feather cues, one hard look, never full easy access. The human unveiled form uses the same world-language but cleaner light and gold transformation mark.

Typography: large readable gothic/woodcut display for headers. Subtitle copy stays legible and minimal. Text must survive thumbnail and print.

Symbol discipline: one primary mark per asset. Do not combine chalice, phoenix, crow, pentacle, runes, tarot numerals, and alchemical glyphs all at once unless the page is explicitly a Codex study spread.
